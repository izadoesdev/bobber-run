Privacy

Bobber Run is a small hobby game. This page says what it stores and why. Last updated 20 October 2026.

Playing offline

Your settings and save game are kept in your browser's local storage. Nothing leaves your computer unless the online features are on.

Playing online

When you open the game you are signed in as a guest automatically. A guest has a random ID and no personal details. While you play online the game stores:

  • your captain name and a cloud copy of your save game;
  • your boat's position, name and paint while you sail, so other sailors can see you. Positions are deleted about a minute after you stop sailing;
  • your best catch of each species, with your captain name. The best ones are shown publicly as world records;
  • what you have given to the coastwide fund;
  • news items about legendary catches and new records, which show your captain name and are deleted after a day;
  • every chat message you send, exactly as you typed it and as other sailors saw it after filtering, with your captain name, account ID, sea and time. Chat is kept permanently so it can be reviewed for abuse, and it is not deleted when you delete your account.

If you make an account, the game also stores your email address and your password. The password is stored only as a salted hash. Your email is used to sign you in and is never shown to other players or used to contact you.

Sign-in tokens are kept in your browser's local storage. The game shows no ads.

Analytics

The game uses Databuddy, a privacy-focused analytics service, to learn how the game is played and where it breaks. It sets no cookies and visitor IDs are anonymized. It records:

  • page visits, load speed and JavaScript errors, with your browser, device type and rough country;
  • game events such as fish caught or lost, purchases, contracts, harbour projects, quests finished, settings changed and screens opened, plus a short progress summary every ten minutes of play;
  • for accounts only, an internal account ID so play on different devices can be counted together.

Databuddy never receives your email, password, captain name or anything you type, including chat.

Who handles the data

  • Convex runs the game's server and database.
  • Railway hosts the web page and keeps standard request logs.
  • Databuddy receives the analytics described above.
  • Stripe handles payments for islands. Your card details go straight to Stripe; the game only keeps which island you bought, the amount and Stripe's payment reference.

How long it is kept, and deleting it

Guests who have not played for 30 days are deleted automatically, with their saves and gifts. World records and chat messages stay. To delete your account, cloud save, captain name, gifts, leaderboard totals and islands, open Online from the title screen or pause menu and press Delete account twice. This device keeps its local save.

Buying an island

Islands are bought with real money through Stripe. Only buy one if you are 18 or older, or a parent or guardian has said yes. An island gives you its name, a small logo and a link on its sign. It is not property: it can't be sold or traded, and it goes back on the market if its owner hasn't played for 60 days. Logos appear to other sailors only after a moderator approves them, and links are checked before they show and again every day. If a payment is refunded, the island goes back on the market.
